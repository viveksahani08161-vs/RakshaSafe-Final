CHAPTER 3
REQUIREMENT AND ANALYSIS
Requirement Analysis
Requirement analysis defines the users, functions, resources and requirements of the Raksha system.
It supports SOS requests, incident and location information, emergency resources, admin
management and centralized data storage. It also covers functional, non-functional, hardware,
software and database requirements.
3.2 Problem Definition
Problem Statement
Users may face emergencies such as harassment, accidents, medical emergencies, fire, floods and
earthquakes where quick and organized information is required. Administrators may also find it
difficult to monitor incidents and maintain updated emergency resources.
Main Problems:
1. Lack of a centralized safety platform.
2. Difficulty recording and tracking incidents.
3. Difficulty finding emergency facilities and resources.
4. Lack of organized incident history.
5. Difficulty managing emergency resources.
6. Lack of centralized admin monitoring.
7. Risk of unauthorized access to sensitive data.
8. Difficulty maintaining unsafe-area and risk information.
9. Difficulty generating useful reports and statistics.
3.2.1 Lack of Centralized Safety Records
Emergency incidents may contain important information such as incident type, description, user
information, location, priority and current status.
If this information is not stored systematically, it becomes difficult to retrieve, monitor and analyse
later.
Raksha provides a centralized database where authorized application components can store and
retrieve emergency incident information.
3.2.2 Difficulty in Sharing Location Information
Location helps identify where an incident occurred. Raksha can request the user’s location and store
available latitude and longitude with the incident. Location accuracy depends on device, GPS, network
and permission availability. If unavailable, the system does not assume a false location.
3.2.3 Difficulty in Finding Emergency Resources
During an emergency, users may need quick information about hospitals, shelters, police stations, fire
stations and relief centres. Raksha keeps these facility details organized, including name, type,
address, contact number, location and operational status. Capacity and operating hours can also be
maintained where available. The system separately stores information about registered response
teams
3.2.4 Difficulty in Monitoring Emergency Incidents
Administrators require a structured method for monitoring emergency incidents.
Raksha provides an administrative dashboard where authorized administrators can:
• View emergency incidents
• View incident details
• View available location information
• Check incident priority
• Check current status
• Assign registered response resources
• Update incident status
• View incident history
• Monitor system activity
The assignment and status features represent software-based coordination and record management.
They do not guarantee physical response from external organizations.
3.2.5 Difficulty in Maintaining Incident History
Emergency incidents may require a chronological record for future reference, reporting and analysis.
Raksha maintains incident updates that record important activities such as:
• Incident creation
• Status change
• Assignment
• Progress update
• Resolution
• Closure
• Cancellation
• Administrative comments
3.2.6 Difficulty in Managing Emergency Resources
Hospitals, shelters, police stations, fire stations, relief centres and response teams may have different
information.
Raksha therefore separates emergency resources into structured database records. Facilities such as
hospitals and shelters are maintained through the Facilities collection, while registered response
teams are maintained through the Rescue Teams collection .Administrators can add, edit, activate or
deactivate resource information according to their permissions. The application displays stored
resource information and does not assume real-time availability unless such information is actually
connected to and maintained by a reliable source.
4.1.11 Notification Management
Notifications keep users informed about important system events such as incident creation,
acknowledgement, assignment, status change, resolution, cancellation, and system updates.
Supported channels may include In-app, Email, SMS, and WhatsApp, depending on configured
services.
Notification records store Incident ID, Contact ID, Channel, Status, Provider Response, Attempt
Count, Last Attempt Time, and Created Date.
Possible statuses are QUEUED, SENT, DELIVERED, FAILED, NOT_CONFIGURED, and
UNAVAILABLE.
3.3 Proposed Solution
Raksha provides a centralized web application that allows registered users to create emergency
incidents and access emergency-resource information.
When an SOS request is submitted, the system creates an incident record and stores the available
incident details in the database. If location permission is granted, available location information is
associated with the incident.
Administrators can monitor incidents through the administrative dashboard, update incident status,
manage facilities and response-team information, and maintain administrative records.
The system can also provide notification functionality through configured communication services.
Notification delivery depends on the availability and configuration of the selected provider.
The system may use a Python-based risk assessment service to process available incident and location-
related factors and provide a risk score and risk level. The risk assessment is intended as software-
based decision support and does not replace professional emergency assessment.
System Limitations
Raksha does not claim to provide
• Automatic police FIR registration
• Guaranteed police response
• Guaranteed ambulance response
• Guaranteed rescue-team response
• Exact rescue ETA
• Guaranteed 24×7 emergency response
• Real-time hospital bed availability without a connected source
• Offline emergency communication unless specifically implemented
Actual emergency response depends on authorized external organizations, service providers, network
connectivity and real-world availability.
